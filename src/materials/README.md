# Materials

Models assemble surfaces from the recipes in `src/materials/`. A material
describes appearance; authored geometry owns shape, topology, face regions
and UV coordinates. Material construction never needs to query a renderer
to discover the underlying 3D shape.

## Material recipes available

- **Oak:** `wood/oakSurface.ts` authors grain, knots, pores, ring flow
  and end grain. `wood/oak.ts` bakes and caches physical-size PBR maps
  through `bakePbrMaps()`. `oakVariant(seed)` selects a repeatable cut.
  `oakBoardFace(seed)` is the quieter planed broad-face variant.
- **Wrought iron:** `metal/wroughtIron.ts` supplies one shared
  `MeshStandardMaterial` for fittings and bands.
- **Blade steel:** `metal/bladeSteel.ts` produces shared, restrained
  carbon-steel face and sharpened-edge materials, with baked PBR maps.
- **Stoneware:** `ceramic/paleStoneware.ts` contains a reusable
  unglazed-clay material family. No current model uses it.

A material does not imply a particular model, and lack of a present
consumer alone is not grounds to classify a general material recipe
as obsolete.

## Texture construction and physical UVs

`toolbox/texturing/bakePbrMaps.ts` samples one surface recipe for three
aligned maps: sRGB color, linear roughness and tangent-space normals.
`tileableNoise`, `fractalNoise`, `flowedGrainField` and the common
field operators are material-independent building blocks.

Construction coordinates are in meters. The baked texture's repeat
transform uses its authored physical tile dimensions. UV offsets belong
on authored geometry: `offsetMeshUvs()` produces an edited
`GeometryResult`, without changing a cached shared texture.

The UV conventions depend on the actual constructor. Do not apply
`BoxGeometry` UV assumptions to authored faces: `boxMesh()`
produces its own physical UVs, `loftPairedSurfaceRows()` retains
perimeter/travel UV coordinates, and `projectAuthoredUvs()`
projects existing authored faces through a specified chart.

## Assign material by authored face regions

`surfaceMesh()` compiles one `GeometryResult` to a Three.js mesh.
Its material mapping follows face-region names generated by the actual
constructor, not hardcoded ideas of which world axis is wood end grain.

For example:

- A board assembled with `loftPairedSurfaceRows()` uses
  `outer`, `inner`, `edge-left`, `edge-right`,
  `cap-start` and `cap-end`. Assign long grain to the first
  four and transverse end grain to the caps.
- Fitted rectangular timbers from
  `models/components/tableTimberGeometry.ts` use `side-0`
  through `side-3` and `cap-start`/`cap-end`. Straight
  `boxMesh()` members use `px`, `nx`, `py`, `ny`,
  `pz`, `nz`. The model's assembly chooses which face is a
  cut end.
- A barrel head from `models/components/cooperedHead.ts`
  now uses `extrudeContour()`: its broad faces are
  `cap-start`/`cap-end`, and its sides are `outer`.
  Do not expect the old `face`, `cut` and `recess` regions.

When tinting with `surfaceMesh({ tint })`, use a vertex-color-aware
material such as `oakVariant(seed).tinted.face` or
`oakBoardFace(seed)`. Vertex colors carry per-piece tonal differences
without spawning unique material/texture copies. The runtime exporter
can merge compatible static materials and pack compatible maps into
PBR atlas pages; unsupported/dynamic meshes retain their own path.

## Repetition and visual inspection

Keep a small deterministic pool of oak seeds across related pieces.
`coherentVariation()` produces spatially related per-piece tones, and
`offsetMeshUvs()` can change the grain window without changing
physical scale or modifying shared texture transforms.

Inspect several adjoining boards and the entire barrel, not just one
texture swatch. A correct map does not guarantee convincing grain
direction, physical contact or the target engine's final appearance.
